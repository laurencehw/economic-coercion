# Appendix B: Analytical Practice and Worked Answers

These problems complement the role-play exercises in [Appendix A](appendix_a_tabletop_exercises.md). They make the two margins operational: calculate what can be substituted, identify who bears the remaining cost, and specify what would establish a policy effect. Numerical inputs are **invented classroom assumptions**, except the explicitly identified reserve-share anchor in Problem 4. They are not estimates of a named country, firm, or policy.

## 1. Classify an Outcome Before Calling It a Success

**Read with:** [Chapter 1](../chapters/chapter_1.md#effectiveness-criteria) and [Chapter 9](../chapters/chapter_9.md#comparative-success-rates-and-measurement-challenges).

A sender restricts equipment exports to compel a target to release journalists. After one year, the target’s equipment costs are 20% higher and production expansion is delayed. No journalists have been released. Exporters have lost sales and an ally is considering withdrawing.

**Task.** Assess the five effectiveness criteria. Identify an outcome measure and a review date before recommending continuation.

**Worked answer.** Compellence has not achieved its stated objective. Higher costs support cost imposition; the delay may support capability degradation if a counterfactual confirms it. Export losses and threatened withdrawal count against collateral damage and sustainability. They do not establish compliance. Track releases by a stated date, verify whether delays exceed an unconstrained comparison case, and evaluate allied licensing decisions. Changing the objective from release to technological denial after seeing the outcome requires an explicit policy revision, not a retrospective success label.

## 2. Calculate the Substitution Margin

**Read with:** [Chapter 2](../chapters/chapter_2.md) and [Chapter 3](../chapters/chapter_3.md).

A manufacturer needs 100 qualified input units each month. A restricted supplier provides 60; unaffected suppliers provide 40. Inventory contains 120 units. Alternative suppliers can add 20 qualified units monthly for months one through three, then 40 from month four. Assume stable demand and deliveries, no losses, no emergency purchases, and no demand reduction.

**Task.** Calculate shortfalls and inventory balances through month five. Explain why announced replacement capacity does not establish resilience.

**Worked answer.** Monthly supply initially equals 40 + 20 = 60, leaving a shortfall of 40. Inventory falls to 80, 40, and zero. From month four, supply rises to 80, still short by 20; output must fall or more supply must be secured. Inventory bridges three months, but substitution does not restore full production. Qualification, deliveries, and downstream use matter more than nominal capacity. Political effects depend on whether the actors bearing these losses can influence the government—the coalitional margin requires separate evidence.

## 3. Distinguish Tariff Incidence from Compliance

**Read with:** [Chapter 6](../chapters/chapter_6.md).

A product originally sells for $100 at the border. A 25% ad-valorem tariff is introduced and the exporter reduces its pre-tariff price to $92. Assume the duty applies to that new price, an unchanged exchange rate, and zero shipping costs and markups.

**Task.** Calculate landed price and tariff payment. Divide the adjustment between importer and exporter. State what this establishes about coercive effectiveness.

**Worked answer.** The duty is 0.25 × $92 = $23 and the landed price is $115. The importer pays $15 more; the exporter receives $8 less. Together the adjustments equal the duty. This describes incidence for the assumed transaction, not total welfare losses: quantities, substitution, and markups can change. It also says nothing about whether the foreign government changed the disputed policy. Compellence requires a political outcome and a causal argument, not just tax revenue or a fall in imports.

## 4. Keep Units and Denominators Separate

**Read with:** [Chapter 7](../chapters/chapter_7.md) and [Chapter 10](../chapters/chapter_10.md).

Suppose a settlement system processes $25 trillion over 250 trading days, while a messaging network sends 50 million messages daily. Separately, use the book’s reported 2025Q3 dollar reserve share of 56.92% as an observed anchor, and assume a future share of 50.5%.

**Task.** Convert annual settlement value to daily value. Explain why it cannot establish market share against the message count. Calculate the reserve-share change in percentage points and in percent relative to the anchor.

**Worked answer.** $25 trillion / 250 = $100 billion daily. Dollars per day and messages per day have different units. Some settlement traffic may also use the messaging network. A comparable share requires a common unit, period, coverage, and overlap rule. The reserve-share change is 50.5 − 56.92 = **−6.42 percentage points**, or −6.42 / 56.92 ≈ **−11.28%** relative to the anchor. That is an assumed future change, not an observed sanctions effect. The anchor’s source and vintage are recorded in Chapter 7; this exercise introduces no new external observation.

## 5. Separate Policy Effects from Before-and-After Changes

**Read with:** [Chapter 4](../chapters/chapter_4.md) and [Chapter 8](../chapters/chapter_8.md).

Investment in a screened sector falls from 10 to 4 units. Investment in a comparison sector falls from 10 to 7 over the same period. A commentator attributes the entire six-unit decline to screening.

**Task.** Calculate the difference in changes. State the assumptions needed for a causal estimate and give two ways the comparison could fail.

**Worked answer.** The changes are −6 and −3, so the difference is **−3 units**. Under parallel trends, no anticipation, and no relevant spillovers, this is consistent with an additional three-unit decline associated with screening. It does not mechanically prove causation. Demand or financing shocks may differ; policy may target firms already declining; or displaced investment may enter the comparison sector. Examine pre-policy trends, composition, timing, and alternative comparisons. The same discipline applies to a technology milestone appearing after export controls: sequence alone cannot distinguish induced innovation from a project already under way.

## 6. Test a Decision’s Dependence on Scenario Weights

**Read with:** [Chapter 10](../chapters/chapter_10.md#scenario-analysis-2035-2050).

Higher scores below mean better outcomes after costs, in arbitrary units. Use A/B/C/D planning weights of 40/25/20/15.

| Strategy | A: Managed competition | B: Economic cold war | C: Regional fragmentation | D: Renewed integration |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Concentrated sourcing | 8 | 1 | 2 | 10 |
| Diversified sourcing | 6 | 6 | 5 | 7 |

**Task.** Calculate weighted scores, worst-case scores, and maximum regret. Move 20 percentage points from B to D and repeat the weighted comparison. Treat weights as assumptions, not calibrated probabilities.

**Worked answer.** Initial weighted scores are **5.35** and **5.95**, respectively. Worst-case scores are 1 and 5. Regret equals the difference from the best strategy in each world: concentrated sourcing has regrets 0/5/3/0 and diversified sourcing 2/0/0/3, giving maximum regrets of 5 and 3. Diversification wins the initial weighted comparison, the worst-case comparison, and the minimum-maximum-regret comparison.

With weights 40/5/20/35, scores become **7.15** and **6.15**. The weighted preference reverses, while worst-case and regret comparisons remain unchanged. A recommendation should state its decision criterion and dependence on weights. An institution willing to accept tail risk for efficiency may choose differently from one whose services must survive the worst reference world. Scenario arithmetic makes that policy disagreement visible rather than resolving it.

## A Reusable Case Assessment

Submit a one-page assessment stating the objective and legal authority; the four descriptive dimensions; the substitution and coalitional mechanisms; an outcome measure and review date; the counterfactual and competing explanations; and costs, relief conditions, and observations that would change the recommendation. Mark facts, causal inferences, and scenario assumptions separately, with a source vintage for empirical numbers. Assess the five effectiveness criteria before assigning one success label.

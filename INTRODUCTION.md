# Economic Coercion

**Author:** Laurence Wilse-Samson
**Affiliation:** NYU Wagner Graduate School of Public Service

> **Revised full draft, last updated November 2026.** One of 22 free, open textbooks I write with AI assistance ([all books](https://lwilsesamson.com/textbooks/)). Corrections are welcome at laurence@lwilsesamson.com.

---

This textbook examines economic coercion by states in the context of strategic competition. It focuses mainly on the US-China rivalry, with applications to other geographies, including US-Soviet Cold War cases and contemporary examples.

Companion: [EconWar](https://econwar.vercel.app), an interactive companion to this book.

## Companion Game

A companion browser-based strategy game, **EconWar**, lets readers play out economic-coercion scenarios from multiple national perspectives, including U.S., Chinese, European, and Global South decision-makers. It applies the frameworks developed in this book.

Play the game: [econwar.vercel.app](https://econwar.vercel.app/)

The game is designed to be used alongside the tabletop exercises in Appendix A, so readers can move from structured classroom simulations to open-ended solo play.

## Purpose

This book serves three audiences:

1. **Policymakers and practitioners** - Practical reference for understanding tools and strategies
2. **Students and educators** - Textbook with exercises and teaching materials
3. **Analysts and researchers** - Data sources, case studies, and analytical frameworks

## Special Features

Each chapter includes:

- **Executive Summary** - Practitioner-focused key takeaways
- **Government Tools Boxes** - Detailed authorities, mechanisms, and examples
- **Perspective Boxes** - Chinese and other international viewpoints
- **Maps and Data Visualizations** - Strategic geography and trends
- **Case Studies** - Real-world applications with lessons learned
- **Tabletop Exercises** - Practical scenario-based learning
- **Data Sources** - Comprehensive guide to databases and tracking tools

## How this book was made

I wrote this book with substantial help from AI language models, principally Claude (Anthropic), which I used for drafting, editing, research, R figure code and tabletop exercises, and consistency checking. The outline, the argument, the selection of material and the judgments throughout are mine, as is the responsibility for what remains wrong. The figures are built from code and public data that readers can rerun; illustrative figures are labelled as such.

In October 2026 I revised the whole text in two further passes, also with AI help: one to remove the stock phrasing that AI drafts produce, and one to bring the prose closer to my own academic writing.

I then had every checkable claim in the text (608 in all: numbers, quotations, attributions, dates and reported findings) compared with a primary source, using AI tools for the lookups and reviewing each change myself. Each claim was confirmed, corrected, softened or cut; the ledger is in `CLAIMS_AUDIT.md` in the book's [repository](https://github.com/laurencehw/economic-coercion). This was a claim-by-claim check, not a line-by-line proofread, and errors surely remain.

Please report errors to laurence@lwilsesamson.com. Corrections usually go live the same day.

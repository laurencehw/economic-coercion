# Preface {-}

## Purpose and Motivation

Economic coercion is now a central feature of 21st-century international relations. Governments increasingly use economic tools to pursue objectives that once required military force or diplomatic pressure alone. Examples include the sanctions imposed on Russia after its invasion of Ukraine, export controls on advanced semiconductors bound for China, the weaponization of the SWIFT financial messaging system, and the strategic dependencies embedded in critical mineral supply chains.

This textbook introduces the theory and practice of economic coercion in an era of intensifying great power competition. It is written for graduate students, policy practitioners, and anyone who wants to understand how economic interdependence, long celebrated as a force for peace and prosperity, has become a source of leverage, vulnerability, and strategic contestation.

I wrote the book because existing treatments of economic statecraft often focus narrowly on sanctions, miss the technological dimension of modern economic competition, or leave out non-Western perspectives, particularly China's strategic thinking about economic security and resilience. I try to fill these gaps with a framework that covers the full range of economic coercion tools, from traditional trade measures to technology controls, and examines both American and Chinese approaches to geoeconomic competition.

## Who This Book Is For

I wrote this textbook for several audiences:

**Graduate Students** in international relations, political economy, security studies, and related fields will find a systematic framework for analyzing economic coercion that draws on international relations theory, economics, and strategic studies. Each chapter includes discussion questions, case studies, and exercises.

**Policy Practitioners** in government, think tanks, and the private sector will find practical analysis of how economic coercion tools work, how effective they are under different conditions, and what strategic considerations shape their use. The book draws on recent policy developments, official documents, and real-world cases.

**Business and Industry Leaders** operating in an increasingly politicized global economy will find how geopolitical competition shapes technology export controls, investment screening, supply chain security, and industrial policy, all of which affect corporate strategy and risk management.

**General Readers** interested in the economic dimensions of U.S.-China competition, the effectiveness of sanctions, or the future of globalization should find the book accessible without loss of analytical rigor.

## Organization and Structure

The role-play exercises in Appendix A are complemented by [Appendix B’s analytical practice and worked answers](../appendices/appendix_b_analytical_practice.md). These problems cover substitution capacity, tariff incidence, financial-system denominators, causal attribution, and decisions under scenario uncertainty. Numerical inputs are labeled classroom assumptions; students should practice separating those from observed facts.

The book has four parts and ten chapters, moving from foundational concepts to contemporary applications and future scenarios:

**Part I: Foundations and Context (Chapters 1-2)**

Chapter 1 sets out the analytical framework for the book. It defines economic coercion and gives the four dimensions along which any case can be described: the domain in which coercion operates (trade, finance, investment, technology), the target (from entire states down to individual officials), the objective (deterrence, compellence, containment, signaling, or regime change), and the intensity of the measures (a spectrum from diplomatic pressure to full economic warfare). A separate set of five criteria (target compliance, capability degradation, cost imposition, sustainability, and collateral damage) is then used to assess whether a given episode succeeds. Chapter 2 turns to global supply chains and critical infrastructure, using semiconductors, critical minerals, and rare earths to show how interdependence becomes a source of leverage and vulnerability.

**Part II: Domains of Economic Statecraft (Chapters 3-5)**

These chapters map the terrain across which economic coercion operates. Chapter 3 covers critical sectors (food, energy, and minerals) and the physical chokepoints that concentrate them. Chapter 4 turns to high-technology competition (AI, quantum computing, biotechnology). Chapter 5 addresses the information, cyber, and digital sphere: telecommunications networks, data governance, and standards-setting.

**Part III: Tools and Mechanisms (Chapters 6-8)**

These chapters cover the specific tools governments use. Chapter 6 covers trade controls including tariffs, quotas, and export controls. Chapter 7 addresses financial statecraft: sanctions, asset freezes, exclusion from payment systems, and central bank measures. Chapter 8 treats investment screening, industrial policy, and strategic assets as both defensive and offensive tools.

**Part IV: Looking Back and Looking Forward (Chapters 9-10)**

Chapter 9 draws lessons from historical cases about what works, what does not, and why. Cases range from Cold War-era grain embargoes to contemporary sanctions on Iran, North Korea, and Russia. Chapter 10 looks forward to how emerging technologies, climate change, and shifting power dynamics will shape economic coercion and geoeconomic competition. A short concluding chapter follows Chapter 10, drawing the book's arguments together and returning to the framework introduced in Chapter 1.

**Appendix A: Tabletop Exercises** collects ten scenario-based simulation exercises (one per chapter) in a standalone appendix. Each runs 60-90 minutes and can be used alongside any chapter.

**Companion Game — EconWar.** A browser-based strategy game, *EconWar* ([econwar.vercel.app](https://econwar.vercel.app/)), accompanies this book. Readers can go beyond the instructor-led tabletop exercises and play out economic-coercion scenarios from multiple national perspectives (U.S., Chinese, European, and Global South), applying the four-dimension framework from Chapter 1. The game works either as a self-directed supplement to the tabletops in Appendix A or as an in-class complement that compresses a full tabletop into a shorter, solo or small-group session.

## How to Use This Book

**For Instructors:**

Each chapter is designed for 1-2 class sessions. Discussion questions at the end of each chapter range from conceptual understanding to policy analysis and current events. Instructors can emphasize particular chapters to suit the course: more time on financial sanctions (Chapter 7) in a course on international political economy, for example, or on technology competition (Chapter 4) in a national security seminar.

The book pairs well with current policy documents, news articles, and academic papers, and supplementary readings are suggested throughout. Case studies can be used for class simulations, debate exercises, or written assignments.

**For Students:**

Each chapter builds on previous material, so I recommend reading sequentially, especially Chapters 1-4, which establish core concepts and domains. Key terms are defined when first introduced and compiled in the glossary. "Chinese Perspective" boxes throughout the book give non-Western viewpoints on economic security, self-reliance, and strategic competition.

The discussion questions ask you to apply concepts to current events and to weigh policy trade-offs. I encourage you to read recent news, policy announcements, and the data sources cited in each chapter alongside them.

**For Practitioners:**

The book can be read selectively by professional focus. Policymakers working on sanctions can focus on Chapters 1, 7, and 9. Those involved in export controls or technology policy will find Chapters 4 and 6 most relevant. Strategists thinking about long-term economic competition should prioritize Chapters 2, 8, and 10.

Each chapter's "Key Insights" section summarizes the main takeaways, and its data sources and references are starting points for further research.

## A Note on Data, Visualizations, and Currency

This book includes 49 original data visualizations covering trade flows, technology competition metrics, sanctions effectiveness, supply chain dependencies, and future scenarios. All visualizations are built from publicly available data, documented in each chapter's "Data Sources" section and in the companion data repository.

Economic coercion changes quickly, and some statistics and policy details will become dated. I have used data current through early 2026, and the analytical framework should hold up as specific policies and numbers change.

All dollar figures are in U.S. dollars unless otherwise noted. When discussing Chinese economic policies and programs, I give both English translations and the original Chinese terms (in pinyin and characters), so that readers can use Chinese-language sources and keep the specific meanings these terms carry in Chinese strategic discourse.

## Analytical Approach and Perspective

The book rests on four analytical commitments:

**Theoretical Pluralism:** I draw on realism, liberalism, constructivism, and political economy, because no single theory fully explains economic coercion.

**Evidence-Based Analysis:** Claims about effectiveness, costs, and strategic dynamics rest on empirical evidence, quantitative data where available, and case study analysis.

**Multiple Perspectives:** The book is written primarily from an American academic perspective, and I incorporate Chinese, European, and Global South viewpoints on economic coercion, self-reliance, and the changing global economic order.

**Policy-Relevant Theory:** The book connects academic analysis to policy practice, covering both the strategic logic of economic coercion and the practical constraints on its use.

## Looking Ahead

Economic coercion is now a central feature of international politics in an era of renewed great power competition, and it is unlikely to recede. Understanding how it works, and how often it fails, has become part of the basic equipment for thinking about global affairs.

This book aims to provide that equipment: a systematic way to reason about the logic of economic coercion, the instruments through which it operates, the conditions under which it succeeds, and the directions in which it is heading. I hope readers come away able to analyze these questions with more precision and less reflexive certainty than the public debate usually allows.
